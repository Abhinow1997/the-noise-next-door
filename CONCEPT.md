# Concept: The Noise Next Door

`the-noise-next-door` · CSYE 7270 · Assignment 2 · Draft v3

## The game in two sentences

You're a raccoon whose quiet corner of the forest is invaded by noisy campers. You plan heists, pick latches, and rig their routines to steal their food and ruin their trip, and when they finally chase you out, your mischief follows you into town.

## Core loop

- **Repeat:** watch what the humans are doing, pick a job from your heist board, and pull it off. Open things with your paws (unzip, unlatch, untie), or rig their stuff so their own routine sets off the prank, then get the loot back to your hiding spot.
- **Decide each time:** when to strike (their routine tells you when they'll be busy), whether to grab now or rig something and wait, and how long to keep fiddling with a latch or zipper while someone might look over.
- **Risk:** suspicion builds in stages: a puzzled "huh?", a look around, the flashlight, a real search. Stay hidden and they talk themselves out of it. Get caught holding their stuff and they take it back and shoo you off. You lose the item and some time, never a finished job.

## Story

- **Opening.** Music, laughter, and car doors wake the raccoon in his tree hole. He looks down at a group of noisy campers who've set up right under his tree, snacks and all.
- **Act 1: The campsite.** He raids their food, rigs their gear, and scares them, while one camper's journal gets more paranoid by the page. Eventually they chase him out of his part of the forest, and as he runs he snatches a last souvenir: that camper's red beanie.
- **Act 2: The town.** He ends up at the edge of town, which is even louder than the campers were but has trash bags everywhere. He works the alley behind a diner and a backyard full of raccoon-proof bins while a neighborhood group chat tracks "the masked bandit in the beanie." His biggest heist, the block party barbecue, ends with animal control catching him.
- **Ending: the ride home.** A photo of the captured bandit goes around town, and the campers recognize their beanie. Animal control can only release him where he came from, and the only people who know where that is are the campers, because they chased him out of it. They drive him back in the same car he raided, open the carrier under his tree, and let him keep the beanie. He climbs into his tree hole, and for the first time in the game, the forest is quiet.
- **Epilogue: here we go again.** Some time later, car doors slam and music starts up: a new group is setting up at the campsite. The last shot mirrors the first, with the raccoon looking down from his tree hole at noisy campers, except this time he pulls on the red beanie and rubs his paws together. The End?

**Sample jobs.** Campsite: steal the marshmallows · make the camper spill their cocoa · swap a marshmallow for a sock before roasting · collapse the tent at bedtime. Town: raid the diner dumpster · beat the bungee-corded bin · steal from the block party barbecue.

## Design pillars

1. **Outsmart the humans.** Every job is solved by reading a human's routine and turning it against them.
   *Visual:* each human's current activity reads at a glance from pose and props (stick raised, mug in hand, trash bag over the shoulder).
2. **Mischief, not malice.** The raccoon is a lovable pest, nobody really gets hurt, and getting caught is slapstick.
   *Sound:* humans grumble, sigh, and yelp instead of shouting, and failure never gets a harsh sting.
3. **Home should be quiet.** The forest is the raccoon's calm, cozy home, and humans are the noise that invades it, first at the campsite and then all over town.
   *Sound:* soft crickets around his tree, with the campers' racket, and later the town's traffic, layered on top as the intrusion.
4. **Every prank lands.** Each finished job gets a clear, funny payoff.
   *Visual and sound:* the job is scribbled off the heist board with a short piano flourish.

## Art direction

Flat shapes: chunky, rounded forms with flat color fills, at most one shadow tone per color, a small palette, and no texture. This keeps each human's activity readable at game size (pillar 1), keeps everyone soft and cartoonish (pillar 2), and lets color tell home from intrusion: soft blues and warm firelight in the forest, harsher white light in town (pillar 3). The raccoon stays lighter than the ground around him, with a pale face mask and cream tail rings, so he never disappears into the dark.

- **Lighting:** a blue-violet moonlit night with warm amber pools from lanterns and the campfire; the deepest shadows are dark teal, never black.
- **Materials and era:** 1970s camping gear simplified to flat shapes (canvas A-frame tent, enamel mugs, plaid blanket, boxy cooler), then a small-town diner with dented metal trash cans and a neon sign.
- **Mood:** a quiet late-night cartoon where everything is round and soft and nothing looks sharp or threatening.

## Audio direction

The player should feel sneaky delight, like they're getting away with something, and failure should sound funny, not punishing.

- **Music:** one playful solo piano loop, light and a little mischievous. It plays softly while you explore, dips lower and muffled when a human gets suspicious, gets louder during a chase, cuts out for a beat when you're caught, and comes back when you retry. Pausing muffles it; finishing the jobs plays a flourish and fades the loop out. The ending brings the theme back slow and gentle for the quiet forest, then the new campers' noise cuts in and the playful version kicks back in as he pulls on the beanie.
- **Sound effects:** short, soft, and cartoony: zippers, latches, rustles, thumps, and clatters. Humans are non-verbal (grumbles, yelps, sighs), so no voice work is needed.
- **Ambience:** crickets and a crackling campfire in the forest; traffic, a distant siren, and a buzzing sign in town, so it feels louder than the home he lost.

## This assignment's slice

The campsite, one camper with one routine (roasting marshmallows and sipping cocoa by the fire), and two jobs: steal the marshmallows by unzipping the backpack, and loosen the thermos lid so the camper spills their cocoa at cocoa time. The raccoon starts at his tree, which is also where he stashes loot.
