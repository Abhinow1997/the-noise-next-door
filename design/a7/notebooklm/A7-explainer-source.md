## The game, in one paragraph

*The Noise Next Door* is a slapstick stealth game made in Godot 4.7.2 (Forward+, GDScript) for CSYE 7270, one game built up one assignment at a time. A raccoon just wants a quiet night in his tree, but a party starts next door at a campsite. The player sneaks the raccoon down to prank the campers: stealing their marshmallows, turning their music off, raiding their cooler, dismantling a tent, raiding a bear bag, and letting their balloons go. The campers have vision cones; if they see him, they take fright, chase him and shoo him away. Assignment 7 is about **animation**.

**The moth's sheet** was generated by the author with Google Gemini, from a prompt Claude Code wrote, and made into a 2 × 2 sheet by a committed script. The script keys out the green and clears the lines between the cells; nothing is moved or resampled. The four cells are wings raised, halfway up, spread flat and angled down. The flutter plays them out and back, 0, 1, 2, 3, 2, 1, one frame each, so the loop is 6/24 s (four flaps a second).

**The moth's job:** on a New Game, once the raccoon is out of his hollow, the moth flutters in and annoys him like a fly. It buzzes round his head and dips as if to land, but never comes within 0.25 m. As soon as he moves, it flies about 3 m ahead of him along the trail to the camp. At the camp it goes to the fire, and is over the flames while they burn.

## The four ideas, each pointed at this project

### 1. What a clip is, and why a track naming a property that doesn't exist fails silently
- **A clip** is an `Animation` resource holding tracks. Each track names a node and a property, bone or blend shape, and holds keys: a time and a value. Playing it sets those values at those times. Nothing in a clip knows about the game.
- **The moth's clip** is one value track on the sprite's `frame` property (`.:frame`), with six keys: cells 0, 1, 2, 3, 2, 1 at 0, 1/24, 2/24, 3/24, 4/24 and 5/24 s. Which picture "cell 2" is depends on the slicing (`hframes` 2 × `vframes` 2), not on the image.
- **The rig's clips** are bone tracks: rotations and positions whose subname is a bone of the `Skeleton3D`.
- **Why it fails silently:** if a track names a property that doesn't exist, Godot plays the clip and simply sets nothing, with no error. The course's track check (`tests/track_check.gd`) branches on track type, because a bone track's subname is a bone, not a property. It found the mutation that renamed the moth's track to `.:fram`: the read-back failed.

### 2. What one authority means, and what happens when there are two
- **One authority:** exactly one thing writes to an animated node. With two writers, the one that runs last in the frame wins, and which one that is depends on node order, which isn't a design.
- **Here:**
  - `scripts/raccoon_model.gd` is the only writer to the rig's player. A test checks that all 5 of its `play()`, `seek()` and `speed_scale` calls are on that player, and that no other script reaches it.
  - `scripts/player.gd` only writes the model's inputs (`jumping`, `landing`, `jump_id`).
  - The model's own leap `seek()` (used for leaps out of the hollow) is kept out of the jump, or it would be a second writer during it.
  - The moth's driver is the only writer to the moth's player.
- **An example of two writers, left as it was:** the dancing campers' players are played by one script and sought to the music's beat by another. That predates this assignment, and it's named in CLAUDE.md as not to be copied.

### 3. What the two clocks are, and which one the game reads on
- **Every clip advances on the process tick** (once a frame).
- **Gameplay reads the jump on the physics tick** (60 a second at every frame rate): takeoff, its phases, the landing and `landed` are all counted in physics ticks. Nothing in the game reads a clip's position.
- **Measured:** the clip shows each change one physics tick after the game, 0.017 s, at 60 and 144 fps. At 30 fps a frame holds two ticks, so it's one or two ticks: **the largest disagreement is 0.033 s.**

### 4. What it means to design feel in frames, not seconds
- **The phase table is the design,** at 24 fps. Frame 0 is takeoff, the physics tick F is seen. For a held jump:

| Phase | Frames | Physics ticks | What the player may do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Startup: the push and rise | 6 | 0–14 | Let go of F to cut the height; steer at reduced air control from tick 1 |
| Active: the top and the fall | 4 | 15–24 | Steer; a press within 5 ticks of landing is kept |
| Recovery: the landing | 12 ticks | landing + 0–11 | A kept or new press starts the next jump at once |

- **The keys are built from the table:** `jump`'s keys sit at frames 0 (the push-off), 3 (stretched up), 6 (gathered at the top) and 10 (front paws reaching down), and `jump_land` lasts 4 frames.
- **Why frames, and why these frames:**
  - at 24 fps only every second frame lands on a 60 Hz physics tick (2 frames = 5 ticks);
  - an early 3-frame crouch would have put takeoff between two ticks, and Claude Code caught that itself before the brief was written.
- **When play says the timing is wrong, the table changes, not the keys,** and the change flows down. The behaviour pins measured the jump before any clip was wired, and four of the brief's worked-out numbers were wrong (for example, a held jump lands 25 ticks after takeoff, not 26). The brief was revised to the measurements.
- **The one tuning revision came from play,** not from Claude's reading. The author saw "The hind legs don't particularly move", so the push-off key was re-posed with the hind legs bunched under the hips. Claude predicted what the next strip would show; the author recaptured it and kept the change after playing it: "Yes, keep r1".

## The raccoon's seven looping clips, brought up to the same standard

idle (4 s), walk (1 s), run (0.667 s), sneak (2 s), bored (4 s), sleep (4 s) and climb (1 s), all at 24 fps, `LOOP_LINEAR`.
- **Each has a written cycle spec,** and for the four gaits, its footfalls in frames.
- **Its seam is checked:** every track's value at 0 matches its value at the clip's length.
- **Loop modes:** they used to be set in code at run time. They now live in the importer's settings (`raccoon_v2.glb.import`), and that was proved by reimporting from an empty cache.
- **Strips:** each loop got a frame strip and the author's verdict, and all seven were kept. Climb is noted for later work. Sleep holds one pose with no breathing, which the author kept.
- **Contact sheets:** every strip, the jump's two (r0 and r1) and each of the seven loops', is laid out as one picture in `captures/sheets/`, frames left to right in the order they were captured, by a committed script (`tools/make_strip_sheets.gd`). `captures/sheets/INDEX.md` says which frame is where. Pixels are copied, never resampled.

- **The graded sprite clip** moved from the balloons to the moth. The author later replaced the pixel-art moth with their own Gemini moth, cut it to one moth, and gave it its job as the guide.
- **The party's sound waves,** which point the way at the edge of the screen, now come in bursts of three every 8 beats instead of all the time.

## What the tests proved, and what they can't

- **Read-backs:**
  - the rig: 51 checks. Every bone track resolves; `step` is exactly 1/24; the loop modes; the jump's keys at its phase frames; each loop's seam.
  - the moth: 22 checks. One discrete value track on `frame`; every key a real cell; the key times; `step`; `LOOP_LINEAR`; the seam rule for a frame track (the last cell differs from the first, and the last gap equals the key interval).
- **Pins:** 26 checks, committed before the change, then re-pinned in their own commit where the brief predicted.
- **State machine:** 89 checks, with real key presses (`Input.parse_input_event`). They read `current_animation` on every tick of a jump, and each of the seven loops plays its own clip.
- **Edges:** every phase boundary, with F let go, F again and a move key, one tick before, on and one tick after, at 30, 60 and 144 fps (239, 238, 238 checks). The first run found three places where the brief promised more than the jump does: air control starts the tick after takeoff; a press is kept only within 5 ticks of landing, not 0.12 s; the peak is on tick 13. The brief was revised; the game stayed as it was.
- **The balloons (not graded):** 35 checks on their clips (`bob`, `inflate`, `float_away`), and 71 on the task, through real key presses: she blows them up, sits on the chair, E lets them go, and she calls the others.
- **Mutations, each in its own git worktree, each failing its test:** a broken track subname, the loop flipped off, a key on a cell the sheet doesn't have, and a reimport from an empty cache (which passes, because the loop modes are in the importer; with them taken out of the importer it fails 7 checks).
- **The baseline:** every earlier test was run before and after the change, each side in a clean worktree from an empty cache, with the same counts. One old test (`cost_effects.gd`) fails 1 of 4 on both sides, so it isn't this change's.
- **What no headless run can show:** whether the motion reads, whether a seam looks clean, how a phase feels. Those are the frame strips (fixture captures from preview scenes, with Movie Maker) and the author's eyes in play. Claude's reading of a strip is a claim, not a check.

## Glossary for the hosts

- **Frame:** one picture of a clip at 24 fps (1/24 s).
- **Physics tick:** one of the game's 60 updates a second.
- **Process tick:** once per drawn frame.
- **`AnimationPlayer`:** Godot's node that plays clips.
- **`Sprite3D` `frame`:** which cell of the sheet shows.
- **`hframes`/`vframes`:** how the sheet is sliced.
- **Committing action:** once started, it plays out (startup, active, recovery).
- **Read-back:** a headless test that loads a clip and checks its structure.
- **Pin:** a test of what the game does now, written before changing it.
- **Mutation:** a deliberate break, to prove a test catches it.
- **Fixture capture:** frames from a preview scene, not from normal play.
