## 2. The Video Overview's settings

Choose **Video Overview**, then **Customize**: the Explainer format, English. Paste one of the prompts below into "What should the AI hosts focus on?".

### The full prompt

> Make an explainer about Assignment 7 (animation) of The Noise Next Door, a Godot 4.7.2 stealth game about a raccoon. Use only Assignment 7. Treat A7-explainer-source.md as the guide; where CHANGE-BRIEF.md shows an older plan, the latest dated revision is what's in the game.
>
> Cover, in this order:
> 1. The game, and its two graded clips: the jump (an imported 3D rig, the committing action, with jump and jump_land) and the moth's flutter (a Sprite3D frame track that loops). Give both jobs in the author's words.
> 2. The four ideas, each tied to a line of this project: what a clip is, and why a track naming a missing property fails silently (the moth's `.:frame` track, and the track check that caught `.:fram`); one authority (raccoon_model.gd, and the moth's driver); the two clocks (clips on the process tick, gameplay on the physics tick, measured as 0.017 s at 60 fps and up to 0.033 s at 30); and feel designed in frames (the 24 fps phase table, with keys at frames 0, 3, 6 and 10).
> 3. The sheet and its slicing (2 × 2, cells 0, 1, 2, 3, 2, 1), and the frame track that reads it.
> 4. The tests: read-backs, pins, edge tests at 30, 60 and 144 fps, and mutations in git worktrees. Then what no headless run can show.
> 5. The tuning revision from play (the push-off, r1), and where the author overruled Claude's strip readings.
> 6. How the plan changed: the jump back to the game's own; the balloons from pixel art to 3D, then a task by the table where the girl sits on the chair (not graded); the moth becoming the guide.
> 7. Human and AI contributions, the sheet's source (generated by the author with Gemini), the limits, and one next step.
>
> Use the exact numbers in the sources; don't invent any. Call the strips fixture captures. Keep it clear and friendly, for a game-development class.
